# Stoked Brotherhood Command Center — Product Vision & Feedback

## The Core Emotional Problem

The app currently feels like a **dashboard**. It needs to feel like a **command center of a brotherhood**. Those are two different emotional experiences.

> "When I log in I should feel 'I'm part of something.' Not 'I'm checking my account.'"

---

## What Is Already Working

- **Visual style**: muted colors, masculine, premium, not trying too hard, plenty of whitespace — closer to Patagonia than Call of Duty. Matches the Stoked brand.
- **Hierarchy**: Kimball → Communicator → XP → Momentum → Weekly Check-in. Natural eye flow.
- **It breathes**: not cluttered. Most dashboards try to show everything. This one doesn't.

---

## Where It Falls Short

### 1. It feels lonely
Brotherhood is supposed to mean "I belong somewhere." But the screen says "Here are YOUR stats." Almost nothing reminds the user that there are hundreds of other men walking beside them.
- Like Strava showing only your own pace
- Like Duolingo without leagues
- Like Peloton without other riders

**The community is missing.**

### 2. There isn't enough emotion
Humans don't wake up excited because they have 100 XP. They wake up excited because:
- "I just passed Josh."
- "I only need 40 XP to reach Initiate."
- "My tribe is winning."
- "I'm on a 27-day streak."
- "My brother commented on my check-in."

Those create dopamine. Numbers alone don't.

### 3. "Profile" is misplaced
Valuable — but not immediately after logging in. Most people don't log in to edit themselves. They log in to DO something. Move Profile to Settings or My Card.

### 4. Weekly Check-In should dominate the page
It's the most important action, yet visually the least exciting thing. If weekly check-in is the heartbeat of Brotherhood, it should FEEL like the heartbeat.

---

## The One Question the Home Screen Should Answer

> **When I log in, I should immediately know: What should I do next?**

Right now the user is looking at information. They should be looking at a mission.

**Instead of** `100 XP` → **Imagine:**
```
Welcome back, Kimball.
Your mission this week:
☐ Weekly Check-In
☐ Finish "Cold Shower" Challenge
☐ Encourage one brother
☐ Read this week's principle
```

Now they've got purpose.

---

## Specific Feature Improvements

### Archetype needs personality
Instead of just `COMMUNICATOR` label, show:
```
COMMUNICATOR
"You naturally unite people through words and relationships."

Strengths: Encouraging · Diplomatic · Influential
Watch Out: Avoiding conflict · Saying yes too often
This Week: Practice difficult honesty.
```
It should feel like a mentor, not a label.

### XP needs meaning through unlocks
XP is exciting in games because it unlocks things. Currently it's just a number.
```
100 XP
↓ Next Unlock: Private Campfire
↓ Next Badge: Firestarter  
↓ Next Rank: Initiate (15 XP needed)
```

### Momentum should be visual
`0.0` doesn't tell much. Use a mountain, flame, arrow, or wave — the stronger the consistency, the stronger the visual becomes. Humans read pictures before numbers.

### Give a reason to come back tomorrow
The home screen should surface live activity:
```
🔥 4 brothers encouraged someone yesterday.
🏔️ New challenge unlocked.
👊 Josh just passed you.
📈 Your tribe moved into 2nd place.
🎯 Only 18 XP until Initiate.
```

### Make it feel alive (subtle animation)
- XP bar animates on load
- Streak flame flickers
- Momentum fills progressively
- A brother pops into the feed
- These tiny things separate premium apps from basic ones

---

## Priority Roadmap (in order)

1. **Mission-driven home screen** — not stats-driven
2. **Surface the brotherhood** — recent activity, tribe progress, social interactions
3. **Give XP meaning** — unlocks, ranks, visible progress toward milestones
4. **Archetype as living coach** — strengths, cautions, weekly guidance
5. **Visual energy** — subtle animations, better iconography, streak/momentum emphasis

## The North Star

> "Stoked's differentiator isn't gamification — it's transformation. Very few apps make a man feel that he's becoming someone better while belonging to a community pursuing the same goal."

Every login should answer three questions:
1. **Who am I becoming?**
2. **What should I do next?**
3. **Who is walking this journey with me?**

If the app answers those three questions every single session, it stops being a dashboard and becomes a daily ritual.
